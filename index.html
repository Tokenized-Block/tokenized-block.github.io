How a Tokenized Block works

A block is bytes you own. You choose a name, a symbol, a supply and a description. The app turns that into a B-20 token — Base's native token standard, where the token is a precompile rather than a deployed contract. Its address always starts with 0xb200, and its code is exactly one byte: 0xef.

It is born finished. In the same single transaction, the block is created, its supply cap is set, and the whole supply is minted to you. There is no window in between. No account holds DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE or MINT_ROLE — not you, not us, not anyone. That is not a promise we make; it is a consequence of the transaction you sign, and you can check it yourself with hasRole on any block.

What that costs you: nothing about the block can ever change. Not its name, not its logo, not its description. Minting one more unit is impossible because the supply already equals the cap and no one holds the role. And you cannot destroy them either: burn needs BURN_ROLE, which nobody holds — so the supply can never go down. Sending units to a dead address takes them out of circulation, but totalSupply does not move. Immutability is the feature and the price, in both directions.

The salt fixes the address. Your block's address is derived from the standard, your wallet address, and the salt you choose. Two different wallets with the same salt get two different blocks. The same wallet with the same salt gets the same address — so a salt you have already used is already taken.

Then it can be traded. A block can be paired with another currency in a Uniswap v4 pool, quoted, and swapped. The app builds every one of those transactions in your browser and shows you the state it reads back from the chain between each step.

This page never holds a key and never holds funds. It encodes a transaction and hands it to your wallet. Your wallet shows it to you, and you decide. Nothing is charged: what you pay is the network's gas, to the network, and nothing else.

⚠️ What is not proven yet. Everything above has been exercised end to end on Base Sepolia — a test network. The encoders are compared byte for byte against the reference toolchain, but nothing here has been used at scale by anyone other than its author. Treat it as working code, not as a finished product.
